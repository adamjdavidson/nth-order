# Methods for Nth-Order Analysis

Use this reference to select and apply methods without turning the executive response into a methods lecture.

## Futures Wheel: map consequences broadly before deeply

A Futures Wheel maps direct and indirect consequences of a central change in successive rings. Use it to generate multiple branches across domains, then decide which are material. Complete one ring before continuing to the next so a compelling single chain does not crowd out alternatives. The UK Government describes it as a structured way to map successive cascades, connections, and causalities; Bengston notes that outer rings become increasingly speculative.

- [UK Government Futures Toolkit — Futures Wheels](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts/the-futures-toolkit-html)
- [Bengston, *The Futures Wheel*](https://www.fs.usda.gov/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2015/nrs_2015_bengston_002.pdf)

Use a fourth ring sparingly. Convert it into a conditional watchpoint with an observable signal instead of a prediction.

## Systems thinking: find the loops that change the decision

Use causal links to identify reinforcing loops, balancing loops, delays, thresholds, and bottlenecks. Keep the map simple: the purpose is to reveal the structure that drives behavior, not to model every connection.

- [MIT Center for Transportation & Logistics — Causal Loop Diagrams](https://ctl.mit.edu/Introduction-causal-loop-diagrams)

## Scenarios: prepare for uncertainty, not a single forecast

Use scenarios only when critical uncertainties produce meaningfully different choices. Define a small set of plausible future conditions, stress-test options against them, and favor actions that remain valuable or preserve flexibility. Do not make scenario likelihood the focal debate unless strong evidence supports it.

- [OECD — Strategic Foresight](https://www.oecd.org/strategic-foresight/)
- [Lempert et al. — Robust Decision Making](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0472)

## Transaction-cost economics: test organization boundaries

Transaction-cost economics is useful when a change affects the cost of discovering counterparts, contracting, coordinating, verifying, monitoring, or adapting across boundaries. Ask whether the economics favor markets, firms, networks, or new hybrids—but do not reduce the answer to transaction costs alone. Account for control, incentives, trust, integration difficulty, regulation, proprietary assets, and power.

- [Coase — *The Nature of the Firm*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2626876)

## Practical quality checks

- Seek effects across more than one lens before deciding a chain is material.
- Name the mechanism between every two claims; remove steps that cannot be explained.
- Include positive, negative, and countervailing effects when they are plausible.
- Prioritize consequences by decision relevance, magnitude, uncertainty, reversibility, and time to act.
- End with actions and signals, not a catalogue of speculative outcomes.
